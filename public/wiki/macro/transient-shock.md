## Overview
The 2026 Strait of Hormuz oil shock sent Brent crude to $118/barrel, generating immense stagflation anxiety. However, this transient shock failed to trigger stagflation. The subsequent collapse in inflation expectations and a disinflationary trend demonstrated the structural resilience of the US economy and a critical shift in Federal Reserve policy execution.

## Geopolitics & The Energy Shock
The conflict between the United States and Iran in early 2026 caused a severe bottleneck at the Strait of Hormuz, shutting in over 10.5 million barrels per day. Brent crude peaked at **$118.03/bbl** in April 2026. The mid-June Geneva peace agreement and aggressive non-Middle Eastern supply responses crashed the market, erasing wartime gains and truncating the supply-side inflation transmission mechanism.

## Deconstructing the Inflation Paradox
The May 2026 CPI report highlighted a stark divergence:
- **Headline CPI** hit **4.2% YoY**, driven entirely by volatile energy (Energy prices rose 23.5% annually).
- **Core CPI** (excluding food and energy) rose a benign **0.2% MoM**, with critical categories experiencing outright deflation (used cars -2.0%, medical care commodities -1.8%).

## Structural Resilience
The US economy has profoundly transformed, dismantling the mechanisms that previously converted oil shocks into stagflation:
- **Declining Energy Intensity**: The economy consumes less than 1/3 of the oil per $1,000 of GDP compared to fifty years ago.
- **Domestic Production Buffer**: The US is now a dominant producer, neutralizing aggregate national employment losses through robust job gains in oil-producing states.
- **Margin Compression**: Corporate America chose to absorb the supply shock, with 80% of firms making little to no change in retail pricing to prevent demand destruction.

## The Collapse of Market Expectations
By late June 2026, breakeven inflation rates across all major time horizons (5-Year, 10-Year, 5Y5Y Forward) systematically collapsed toward the Fed's **2.0%** target, effectively validating the transient nature of the shock.

## The "Warsh Effect"
Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh implemented a severe, uncompromising shift in monetary policy execution defined by austere communication, the death of forward guidance, and a rigid adherence to absolute price stability.

## Stagflation vs. Demand Destruction
Structural labor constraints prevented demand-driven inflation:
- **Stagnant Real Wage Growth**: Real hourly earnings decreased by 0.09% in May.
- **Low-Hire, Low-Fire Equilibrium**: Job growth slowed to 22,500/month, severely restraining consumer confidence and money velocity.
