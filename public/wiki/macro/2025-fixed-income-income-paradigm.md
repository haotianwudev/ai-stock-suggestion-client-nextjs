## Overview

2025's long-term fixed income market marks a paradigm shift: total returns are dominated by coupon income rather than price appreciation, set against a "stagflation-lite" backdrop of decelerating growth, sticky inflation, and an anticipated Federal Reserve easing cycle. The core investor imperative is to lock in historically high yields in quality assets before that easing cycle compresses them.

## Key Concepts

- **Income-driven returns** — H1 2025 broad index returns of 4-7.25% are driven primarily by coupon payments, not falling rates; high-yield bonds offer historically attractive ~7.5% yields.
- **"Stagflation-lite"** — 2025 GDP growth projected at 1.6% (down from ~2.5% in 2024) alongside stubborn headline CPI around 2.9%, driven by base effects and resilient services inflation. Growth slows enough to justify Fed cuts without tipping into recession.
- **Bull steepening** — the consensus yield curve scenario: short-term rates fall faster than long-term rates as the market prices in near-term Fed cuts, while longer-dated yields stay elevated on inflation risk and heavy Treasury issuance to fund fiscal deficits.
- **Stock-bond correlation normalization** — after years of positive correlation driven by inflation shocks, bonds are expected to resume their classic diversifying role as growth (not inflation) becomes the dominant market driver, sending capital from equities to government bonds during downturns.

## Sector Positioning

- **Investment Grade Corporates — Favorable.** Healthy balance sheets, but tight spreads offer little compensation for risk; favor non-cyclical sectors and issuers with strong pricing power.
- **High-Yield Corporates — Cautious/Underweight.** Attractive headline yields don't fully compensate for rising default risk in a decelerating economy; highly selective approach required.
- **Municipal Bonds — Highly Favorable.** Compelling tax-equivalent yields, strong credit fundamentals, and relative insulation from federal policy headwinds make this a standout opportunity.

## Core Strategies for 2025

1. **Lock in high yields** — extend duration out of cash to capture 5%+ yields in high-quality assets before the market fully prices in future cuts.
2. **Prioritize quality** — favor investment-grade bonds; lower-quality credit spreads don't adequately compensate for rising default risk.
3. **Embrace intermediate duration** — position in the 2-10 year "belly" of the curve to benefit most from Fed cuts while limiting exposure to long-term fiscal uncertainty in the 20-30 year range.
4. **Leverage municipal bonds** — for taxable investors, tax-equivalent muni yields are among the most attractive in years.

## Key Takeaways

- The defining shift in 2025 fixed income is that coupon income, not price appreciation, is doing the work — a structurally different return driver than the prior decade of near-zero rates.
- Bull steepening is a base case built on two forces pulling in opposite directions: near-term Fed cuts pressuring the short end down, and fiscal/inflation concerns keeping the long end elevated.
- The time-sensitive imperative is locking in today's high starting yields in quality assets before an anticipated easing cycle compresses them.
