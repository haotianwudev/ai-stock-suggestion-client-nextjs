## Overview
The bond term premium represents the excess yield investors demand for holding long-duration sovereign debt rather than rolling over short-term risk-free instruments. This guide covers the theoretical foundations, econometric modeling, macroeconomic drivers, and strategic portfolio applications of the term premium.

## 1. Theoretical Genesis
- **The Pure Expectations Hypothesis:** Suggests that long-term rates are simply the average of expected future short-term rates. This hypothesis is routinely rejected empirically.
- **The Term Premium:** Risk-averse investors demand compensation for duration risk, inflation uncertainty, and supply-demand imbalances.
- **Decomposition:** 
  $$ \text{10-Year Yield} = \text{Expected Path of Short Rates} + \text{Term Premium} $$

## 2. The QE Era (2010–2022)
- **Central Bank Intervention:** Massive Quantitative Easing (QE) programs extracted duration risk from the market.
- **Term Premium Compression:** This mechanically pushed term premia to historic lows, bottoming out near $-1.50\%$. 
- **Bonds as Hedges:** Low and stable inflation allowed bonds to act as reliable equity hedges, further depressing the required compensation for holding them.

## 3. The Regime Shift (Post-2022)
- **The Return of Inflation:** Sticky inflation and aggressive monetary tightening disrupted the low-volatility regime.
- **Correlation Flip:** Stock-bond correlations flipped from negative to positive, meaning bonds lost their hedging properties and investors demanded higher yields.
- **Fiscal Dominance:** Persistent U.S. primary deficits (near 3.5% of GDP) combined with Quantitative Tightening (QT) created a structural supply-demand imbalance.
- **AI Supply Shock:** Trillions of dollars in AI infrastructure capex financing flooded the market with long-dated corporate and sovereign paper, putting further upward pressure on yields.

## 4. Modeling the Premium: The ACM Framework
- The Adrian-Crump-Moench (ACM) model is the industry standard for estimating the term premium. 
- It uses an affine term structure model to extract the unobservable term premium from the observable yield curve using principal component analysis and cross-sectional regressions.
- **Current Outlook:** The structural premium is forecast to settle in the $1.00\% - 1.50\%$ range through 2026-2030, restoring the traditional upward-sloping yield curve.

## 5. Portfolio Implications
- **Reevaluating 60/40:** With positive stock-bond correlations and higher term premia, traditional 60/40 portfolios suffer.
- **TIPS:** If nominal term premia compress but inflation volatility remains high, TIPS (Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities) offer mechanically superior purchasing power protection.
- **Floating-Rate Credit:** Investors should diversify beyond traditional duration by incorporating floating-rate or alternative credit strategies.
