## Overview

"Insider trading" has two entirely different meanings: a routine, disclosed activity where executives and directors legally buy or sell stock in their own company, and a form of securities fraud involving trades made on material non-public information (MNPI). The legal version, properly read through SEC filings, is also one of the more interesting market-sentiment signals available to investors — because insiders sell for many reasons, but they buy for essentially one: they believe the price will rise.

## Key Concepts

- **Legal vs. Illegal Insider Trading** — the dividing line is whether the trade was based on MNPI. Legal trading uses public information for financial planning or diversification and is mandatorily disclosed via SEC Forms 3, 4, and 5. Illegal trading exploits confidential information (unannounced earnings, pending M&A, regulatory news) in breach of a duty of trust and is prohibited under Section 10(b) and Rule 10b-5.
- **Form 4** — the most important filing for investors: a report of any insider transaction, filed within two business days. Its speed makes it a near-real-time window into insider sentiment.
- **Rule 10b5-1 plans** — pre-arranged trading plans that let insiders trade on a schedule set before they had any MNPI, providing an affirmative legal defense. 2023 amendments added mandatory "cooling-off" periods between plan adoption and the first trade, restrictions on overlapping plans, and enhanced disclosure — closing loopholes that had let some insiders effectively still trade opportunistically.

## Insider Buying as a Sentiment Signal

- **Open-market purchases** using personal funds show the strongest conviction, since insiders are voluntarily putting their own money at risk.
- **Cluster buys** — multiple insiders buying around the same time — suggest confidence is widespread within the company, not just one executive's opinion.
- **Contrarian buying** — insiders purchasing against negative market sentiment or after a significant price drop — is considered a particularly strong signal since it goes against prevailing narrative.
- **Empirical evidence** — portfolios that mimic insider purchases have been shown to earn abnormal returns over time, and aggregate market-wide insider buy/sell ratios have some power to predict broad market direction. The signal tends to be stronger for smaller, less-followed companies where information is less efficiently priced in.

## Landmark Cases (Cautionary Tales)

- **Martha Stewart / ImClone** — tipping and obstruction; Stewart was convicted not for the trade itself but for lying to investigators about it, highlighting "tippee" liability and the severe risk of obstructing a federal investigation.
- **Raj Rajaratnam / Galleon Group** — an organized insider network; exposed the scale of modern systemic insider trading and established wiretaps as a key SEC/DOJ enforcement tool.
- **Jeffrey Skilling / Enron** — fraud-related selling at inflated prices while knowing financials were falsified; led directly to the Sarbanes-Oxley Act of 2002, which overhauled corporate governance and disclosure requirements.

## Limitations and Evolving Tactics

- Insider signals are noisy, not infallible — predictive power concentrates in smaller, less-efficiently-priced stocks, and naive strategies risk data-snooping bias.
- Sophisticated insiders adapt: some now trade on public attention cycles (selling during retail hype, buying as attention fades) or trade in economically linked firms (a supplier's or customer's stock) to sidestep their own company's trading restrictions.
- The two-day Form 4 window, meant to increase transparency, has a side effect: it lets insiders monitor each other's trades in near real-time, potentially enabling implicit coordination or herd-like behavior that amplifies their collective information edge.

## Key Takeaways

- Sell transactions are ambiguous (diversification, taxes, liquidity needs) — buy transactions are the higher-signal event because there's really only one common reason to buy.
- Open-market purchases, cluster buys, and contrarian buying are the three patterns worth weighting most heavily when screening Form 4 data.
- The insider-trading signal works best as a screen for smaller-cap, less-covered stocks, not as a standalone strategy on heavily analyzed large caps.
- Legal insider trading and insider trading fraud are governed by completely different rules and consequences — always check whether a trade falls under proper Form 3/4/5 disclosure before treating any pattern as informative.
