## Overview

A curated reading list spanning two categories: investing & finance books that build a value-investing framework, and life & mindset books that build the psychological discipline needed to apply it. The organizing idea across both lists is that investing success is less about technical knowledge and more about behavior, patience, and clear thinking.

## Investing & Finance

- **The Psychology of Money** (Morgan Housel) — how people actually think about money; success is about behavior, not knowledge.
- **Richer, Wiser, Happier** (William Green) — interviews with investors like Munger, Pabrai, and Templeton distilled into lessons on rational, patient thinking.
- **The Most Important Thing** (Howard Marks) — second-level thinking, understanding risk, and learning from market cycles.
- **Good Stocks Cheap** (Kenneth Jeffrey Marshall) — an actionable value-investing framework integrating finance, strategy, and psychology.
- **Education of a Value Investor** (Guy Spier) — a personal journey from hedge-fund aggression to disciplined, Buffett-inspired value investing.
- **Little Book of Behavioral Investing** (James Montier) — the psychological biases (emotion, overconfidence) that sabotage investors.
- **Mastering the Market Cycle** (Howard Marks) — positioning a portfolio based on where the market cycle currently stands, not on prediction.
- **Poor Charlie's Almanack** (Peter D. Kaufman) — Charlie Munger's talks and his &ldquo;latticework of mental models&rdquo; for rational decision-making.

## Life & Mindset

- **Same as Ever** (Morgan Housel) — timeless truths about human nature (greed, fear, risk) that persist even as technology changes.
- **Atomic Habits** (James Clear) — how small, 1% improvements compound into large results through reliable systems.
- **Ego Is the Enemy** (Ryan Holiday) — ego as the primary obstacle to mastery, and why humility and resilience matter at every career stage.
- **The Subtle Art of Not Giving a F*ck** (Mark Manson) — choosing what to care about and finding meaning in struggle rather than chasing fleeting happiness.
- **Stolen Focus** (Johann Hari) — the external forces (technology, diet) eroding our attention, and how to reclaim it.
- **Clear Thinking** (Shane Parrish) — mental models and practical advice for recognizing and overcoming your own biases.

## Key Takeaways

- The list deliberately pairs investing-specific books with general life/mindset books — the implicit argument is that the psychological discipline (patience, humility, clear thinking, habit-building) is the actual bottleneck, and the investing-specific frameworks only work once that foundation is in place.
- Several authors and themes recur across both categories (Housel appears twice, behavioral psychology threads through Montier, Marks, and Parrish), suggesting the curators see this as one coherent body of thought rather than two separate reading tracks.
- Howard Marks is the only author represented twice within a single category (The Most Important Thing and Mastering the Market Cycle), signaling his cycle-awareness and risk framework are treated as particularly load-bearing for the list's overall philosophy.
