## Overview

ES (E-mini S&amp;P 500) and NQ (E-mini Nasdaq-100) futures are the most liquid derivatives tracking the broad US equity market, trading nearly 24/5. The launch of "Micro" contracts (MES, MNQ) at 1/10th the size democratized access that was historically institution-only, but the same leverage that makes futures capital-efficient also makes them structurally dangerous for undercapitalized traders.

## Key Concepts

- **Contract Specs** — ES: $50 multiplier per index point, $12.50 per 0.25-point tick. NQ: $20 multiplier per index point, $5.00 per 0.25-point tick (NQ moves faster than ES).
- **Micro Contracts (MES/MNQ)** — 1/10th the size of the standard E-mini ($5/point vs. $50/point for ES), enabling strategies to be tested with $500-$1,000 accounts instead of institutional-scale capital.
- **Market Ecology** — three participant types: Hedgers (institutions buying insurance, e.g. shorting NQ to protect a long equity book), Arbitrageurs (HFT algorithms keeping futures price locked to the cash index), and Speculators (retail/prop traders providing liquidity to hedgers).
- **Globex Session** — futures trade nearly 24/5 (6:00 PM Sunday to 5:00 PM Friday ET), with a daily maintenance break (4:15-4:30 PM ET) and the most volatile window at the 9:30 AM US cash open.
- **Section 1256 Tax Treatment** — US futures traders get 60% of gains taxed at long-term rates and 40% at short-term rates regardless of actual holding period, a structural tax advantage over stock trading.

## Critical Pitfalls

- **The Margin Call Trap** — low day-trading margins (e.g. ~$500 for ES) let undercapitalized accounts enter; a normal 10-point intraday move can wipe out that entire margin.
- **Over-Leverage** — controlling $200,000 of notional stock exposure with $10,000 cash means a healthy 5% market correction produces a 100% account loss.
- **Contract Rollover** — ES and NQ expire quarterly (March, June, September, December); volume migrates to the new contract about a week before expiry, and trading the expiring contract past that point means trading into a liquidity vacuum.

## Utility Beyond Trading

Even non-traders use ES/NQ as dashboard indicators:
- **Overnight Sentiment** — checking ES before the market opens signals how a 401k/portfolio will likely perform that day.
- **Sector Rotation Hints** — NQ (tech-heavy) diverging from RTY (small caps) signals a shift toward safety in large-cap tech versus risk-taking in smaller companies.

## Key Takeaways

- Futures are a zero-sum game before commissions — every dollar won is a dollar lost by a counterparty.
- Micro contracts genuinely lowered the capital barrier to futures trading, but low margin requirements are a trap, not an invitation to under-capitalize a position.
- Quarterly rollover and the Section 1256 tax treatment are structural mechanics unique to futures that don't exist in equities.
- ES/NQ function as a real-time economic dashboard even for people who never place a trade.
